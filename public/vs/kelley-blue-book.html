EV Lineup vs Kelley Blue Book

A fair comparison of EV Lineup and Kelley Blue Book for electric car research — what each does best, and when to use the other one.

Last updated October 21, 2026

What each one is

EV Lineup

EV Lineup is a free electric vehicle database and comparison site for anyone shopping for or researching an EV. It holds a standardized spec sheet for 87 electric models from 32 brands — price, EPA range, 0–60 mph, power, battery size, DC fast-charging speed, seats and trims — plus profiles for 35 manufacturers, side-by-side comparisons of any two models, and best-of lists generated from the same data. It is free, needs no account to browse, and has no dealer lead forms.

Kelley Blue Book

Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com) is best known for vehicle valuations. As of October 2026, per its website, it publishes Blue Book values for new and used cars, trade-in and private-party estimates, expert and owner reviews, and dealer listings across all fuel types. See Kelley Blue Book for its own description.

Where Kelley Blue Book is stronger

  • Vehicle valuations and trade-in estimates — the thing KBB is famous for, and something EV Lineup deliberately does not attempt.
  • Resale-value and ownership-cost guidance, useful when an EV's depreciation is the deciding factor.
  • Dealer listings and shopping tools for buying the car once you have chosen it.
  • All-fuel coverage, including hybrids, for shoppers still weighing an EV against a gas car.

Where EV Lineup is stronger

  • A single standard spec table for every EV, including battery capacity in kWh and maximum DC charging speed — fields general car sites often omit.
  • Comparison pages for any two models in the database, with the better figure in each row marked.
  • Data-driven lists such as /best/longest-range-evs and /best/3-row-evs that re-rank themselves from the database.
  • Free, no sign-in, no lead capture, and a documented public API for developers and AI assistants.

Which should you use?

Use KBB when the question is what a car is worth — new, used or as a trade-in. Use EV Lineup when the question is which EV has the range, charging speed, seats or price you need.

In practice the two are complements rather than substitutes: shortlist on EV Lineup, then check the car with Kelley Blue Book before you buy.
